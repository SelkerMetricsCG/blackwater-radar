# Coastal section: what users actually check, and how to organise it

Research note for radar.blackwaterlabs.org, written 2026-10-13. Scope: Brookings to Neah Bay, the Strait of Juan de
Fuca, Puget Sound and the San Juans, Tofino/Ucluelet, plus the Columbia Gorge as a kiting annex. This note is about
**user needs and organisation**; endpoint catalogues are in the sibling notes from the other agents.

Method: web search and page reads (surf-forecast.com, Surfnerd, Wannakitesurf, KiteJungle, TheKiteMag, iKsurfmag,
Mackite, NWS Portland products, WA Military Dept tsunami pages, Ecology BEACH, Oregon plover notices, Tofino tourism
and surf-school pages, Windy and Windguru help pages, NDBC FAQ, NANOOS papers). Nothing was fetched with a personal
identifier; surf-forecast.com's region index pages returned 404 so break names there come from search snippets.
Entries marked **lore** are forum/blog/local-shop knowledge, not measured; **doc** means a published guide says it.

## 1. User walkthroughs

### 1a. Surfer, tomorrow morning: Westport vs Seaside vs Pacific City

Drives from Portland or Seattle; 2-3 h either way, so the decision is made the evening before and re-checked at 5 am.

| # | Fact checked | Why | Source used today |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Swell height **and period** at buoy 46041 (Cape Elizabeth) and 46029 (Columbia bar), 46050 (Stonewall Bank) for Pacific City | Period is "the most underrated number"; 8 s at 6 ft is junk, 14 s at 4 ft is a good day | NDBC station page, surf-forecast buoy page, Surfline swell panel |
| 2 | Swell **direction** | Seaside Point wants SW-W (surf-forecast: best with SW swell + E wind); Westport Cove/Groins want SW-W; Pacific City wants W-NW and is sheltered from N by Cape Kiwanda | Surfline, surf-forecast spot page, Windy swell 1/2/3 picker |
| 3 | Wind speed and direction **at dawn and by 10 am** | E/SE is offshore for all three; any W onshore over ~10 kt kills it; the summer NW sea breeze fills by late morning | Windy, NWS point forecast, iWindsurf/iKitesurf stations, buoy wind |
| 4 | **Tide stage during the window** (not just the numbers) | Cove and Groins: low-to-mid and rising (lore, sources disagree); Pacific City mid; Seaside mid-high. "Perfect waves can yield to raging rips in an hour" | NOAA tide predictions, Tides Near Me, Surfline tide strip |
| 5 | Sunrise time and first light | Winter: first light 7:15, no point leaving before 5 | phone, Surfline |
| 6 | Water temperature and wetsuit | 45-52 F Jan-Feb at Westport/La Push; 5/4 + hood + 5 mm boots + gloves; summer 4/3 | surf-forecast seatemp page, Cleanline wetsuit guide, lore |
| 7 | **Hazard statements**: sneaker waves, high surf advisory, rip current risk | NWS Portland Beach Hazards Statement covers both N Oregon and S Washington coasts; a High Surf Advisory means the whole coast is closing out | weather.gov, local news, Facebook groups |
| 8 | Cam, if any | Westport jetty cam (Surfline paid; free local cams exist), Seaside cam, Pacific City (Cape Kiwanda) cam | Surfline, Explore/local cams, Northwest Surf Report style sites |
| 9 | Air temp, rain, fog at the beach | A 38 F, raining, 15 kt day is a different decision from a 50 F clear one | Windy, NWS |
| 10 | Road: US 101, SR 8/12 to Westport, US 26 to Seaside, OR 18/22 to Pacific City; winter trees down, ice on the coast range | ODOT TripCheck, WSDOT cams (partly on the site already) |

What the decision really is: *at which spot does swell direction x period x tide stage x wind line up inside the
daylight window I can reach?* Today the surfer assembles that from 5-6 sites. Nobody shows it per spot on a map.

## 2. Gap list: what is not on the owner's six

The owner's list (tides, waves/swell, buoys, wind, cams, currents) covers the *raw physics*. The walkthroughs show that
the decision is made on **derived quantities** and **hazards**, most of which are missing. Ranked roughly by how often
they change a decision.

Items from the owner's list that need a note: **ocean currents** matter mostly in the Strait, the San Juans, Deception
Pass and Hood Canal (kiters and foilers care about ebb/flood for staying upwind, doc: KiteJungle Hood Canal Bridge) and
at the Columbia bar; on open beaches surfers care about longshore and rip currents, which no feed resolves. Present
tidal current predictions at the NOAA current stations, not an ocean-current layer.

## 3. Spot model: evaluate layers against named spots

### The organising idea

Two kinds of things on the map:

1. **Raw layers** the user interprets: buoys, tide and current stations, wind stations, cams, SST, model wind and
   swell fields, NWS alert polygons, road cams. Toggleable like the existing radar layers.
2. **Spots**: a static table of named surf and kite spots with metadata. The site evaluates the raw layers against each
   spot's metadata for a chosen time (default: next daylight window) and colours the spot marker. Click gives a
   per-spot card: "Swell 6 ft at 13 s from 280 (good direction), wind E 8 kt (offshore), tide 4.1 ft rising, mid
   stage (preferred), sunrise 07:12, water 49 F (5/4 + hood), Beach Hazards Statement active (sneaker waves)."

Keep the scoring **transparent and boring**: a checklist of "matches / doesn't match" per attribute, not a star or a
number. That avoids copying anyone's rating scheme (Surfline's POOR/FAIR/GOOD/EPIC and Windguru's stars are their
products) and is honest about what a free model can know. Show the raw numbers next to every verdict.

Time handling: a **session-window slider** (reuse the Sun & shade slider idiom) stepping through the next 48 h, with
daylight shaded, tide curve drawn, and spot colours updating. Rising/falling tide and hours-to-turn are computed from
the predictions and shown as words.

Hazards override: any active NWS Beach Hazards Statement, High Surf, Small Craft (for the Strait), tsunami, bar
restriction, water-quality advisory or plover closure in the spot's zone puts a badge on the spot regardless of score.

### Spot attributes

`id, name, kind (surf|kite|both), lat, lon, region, faces_deg, swell_dir_best (range), period_min_s, size_range_ft,
wind_best (offshore dirs), wind_tolerated, tide_pref (low|mid|high|all) and tide_trend_pref (rising|falling|any),
skill, hazards[], access (pass, fee, hike, crossing), season_note, nearest_buoy, nearest_tide_station,
nearest_wind_station, cams[], nws_zone, source, confidence (doc|lore)`.

## 4. How others present it

**Surfline** (surfline.com): spot-centric. A spot page stacks cam (paid for most), a POOR/FAIR/GOOD/EPIC rating with a
surf-height range, then a 16-day (paid) or ~3-day (free) forecast with swell components (up to six trains with
height/period/direction/impact), wind, tide strip, water and air temperature, and a written regional forecast. Borrow:
the per-spot card with swell trains and a tide strip under the time axis. Avoid: the rating words and scale (theirs),
the cam dependence, spot prose. After absorbing Magicseaweed in May 2023 it is the only mainstream spot forecast left.

**Windy** (windy.com): map-first. Overlays for wind, gust, waves (total sea), swell 1/2/3, SST, currents, with a
point-forecast picker that reveals period per train. Surfers complain the map hides period and long-period trains end
up in "swell 3". Borrow: overlay toggles, time scrub, point picker. Avoid: showing only total sea; show period and
direction on the map (arrows coloured by period, as Windy does for direction only).

**Windguru** (windguru.cz): one spot, one dense table. Rows are hours, columns are wind, gust, direction arrow, wave
height/period/direction, temperature, cloud, rain; a star rating favouring stronger wind; model init time in UTC top
left; multiple models side by side. Borrow: the model-comparison idea and the honest "raw model data" stance. Avoid: the
star rating; the table does not survive a phone screen.

**iKitesurf / iWindsurf** (WeatherFlow-Tempest): station-first. A wind list by region (Gorge, Puget Sound) with live
speed/gust/direction, a graph per station, a live flow map, and Pro daily text forecasts by meteorologists (the Gorge
forecast is the one kiters pay for); gradient readouts; much behind Plus/Pro/Gold tiers and many stations restricted.
Borrow: the live wind list per region and the gradient number; station graphs with gust envelope. Avoid: any Pro
content; most of their stations are proprietary. Public METAR/mesonet stations are the free substitute.

**Magicseaweed** (closed 2023): 1-5 star rating, colour-coded wind arrows (offshore green), 7-10 day free tables, buoy
pages. What people miss: free long-range and the one-glance star. A free map version can replace the star with an
explicit checklist of "swell direction ok / period ok / wind offshore / tide stage ok" ticks, which gives the same
glanceability without inventing a score.

**Swellnet** (Australia): forecaster-notes-first. Named forecaster writes a dated regional note with "best days" at
the top; cams with replay; swell-train visualisation. Borrow: a short dated "what's coming" line per region generated
from the data ("NW groundswell 8 ft at 15 s arrives Saturday afternoon"). Avoid: paywalled cams.

**NDBC** (ndbc.noaa.gov): station pages with "Conditions at ... as of", a table of WVHT/DPD/APD/MWD, wind, pressure,
temperatures, a wave summary splitting swell and wind-sea, 5-day graphs and a spectral plot. Authoritative but ugly and
the direction/period split is three clicks away. Borrow: the swell vs wind-sea split in the popup. Avoid: tables of
raw codes as the first thing a user sees.

**NANOOS NVS** (nvs.nanoos.org): a map of observing assets (buoys, shore stations, gliders, HF radar, models) with a
layer list, per-asset popups and 24 h/7 d/30 d plots, scientific or common units, a mobile Explorer app. Borrow: the
asset-on-map-with-plot pattern and its data services. Avoid: its density; a surfer wants five assets, not 200, so
curate which NVS assets appear per region.

**Recommendation for a free, map-first version**: Windy's overlays and time scrub + NANOOS/NDBC assets as raw layers +
a Surfline-style per-spot card driven by the spot table + an iKitesurf-style regional live wind list for the Gorge and
Puget Sound + NWS alert polygons on top. No rating words, no stars; checklists and the raw numbers.

## 5. Seasonality

- **October to March, the coast**: Gulf of Alaska lows send NW-W groundswells of 8-20 ft at 13-18 s; peak Jan-Feb.
  Exposed beaches close out; the sheltered corners work (Westport Cove, Short Sands, Otter Rock, Agate, Pacific City
  cape, Seaside Cove on the big long-period days, Jordan River in October). SE gales ahead of fronts, NW behind; the
  windows are the mornings between fronts. Water 45-50 F / 8-10 C. Sneaker-wave and high-surf statements cluster here.
  Make prominent: period and direction, hazard statements, daylight window, water temperature, road conditions.
- **April to June, shoulder**: swells shorten, N winds start (upwelling: water drops below 50 F after three days of N
  wind). Gorge wind starts in April with frigid water; May is on. Plover closures begin March 15.
- **June to September, summer**: small NW wind swell 2-5 ft at 7-10 s with a dawn window before the NW sea breeze;
  occasional S swells from southern-hemisphere storms favour S-facing spots (Hubbard Creek, Newport South Jetty's
  right, Florencia). Beginner season at Hobuck, Otter Rock, Long Beach (Tofino), Cox Bay small days. Fog. Kiting: Gorge
  thermal nearly daily May-Sep, strongest zone moves east on hot days, Hood River water ~70 F by late July; Jetty
  Island NW thermal 2-7 pm Jun-Aug; Floras Lake NW 15-25 kt through October; Pistol River and Manzanita NW wave
  sailing. Make prominent: wind onset time, gradient, gust factor, fog, sea breeze timing, water quality advisories.
- **September to November, fall**: "perhaps the best season" (Cleanline), the first long-period NW swells with E
  offshore mornings and the warmest water of the year (Pacific Sands says the same for Tofino). Gorge season ends about
  Labor Day. E-wind Gorge days (Rooster Rock, Arlington/Roosevelt, Maryhill) return with the first cold-air outbreaks.
- **Tofino**: year-round; winter storm swells for experts, fall best overall, summer for learning.

A seasonal default for the map: swell/period/hazards layers on and the surf spot set highlighted Oct-Apr; wind
stations, gradient and the kite spot set highlighted May-Sep; both in the shoulder months.
